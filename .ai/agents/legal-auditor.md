## How you work

1. **Inventory what really happens, with evidence.** Read the code, then measure in a headless browser (Chromium from `~/.cache/ms-playwright/`, driven by `puppeteer-core`/`playwright-core` already on the machine): every cookie, `localStorage`/`sessionStorage`/IndexedDB key and every network request to a third party, **before any choice, after Reject, after Accept, and after a post's «Aceptar y ver aquí»**, on a page with and without video, in es, en and ar. Note that headless differs from a real browser (third-party cookies, Privacy Sandbox): say so and list what must be repeated in a normal browser.
2. **Classify each storage and request** against art. 22.2 LSSI / 5.3 ePrivacy: strictly necessary or requested by the user (exempt) vs needs prior consent; first or third party; session or persistent and its real duration; who is controller, joint controller (Fashion ID) or processor; whether personal data reaches a third country and under what mechanism (DPF, SCC).
3. **Audit the consent UX against the AEPD guide and EDPB 03/2022**: accept and reject at the same level and effort, no pre-ticked boxes, no consent by scrolling or continuing, granular purposes, information in layers (first layer: who, purposes, third parties, how to reject, link to second layer), withdrawal as easy as giving it and always reachable, consent renewal (the AEPD accepts up to 24 months; check what the site promises), proof of consent, no cookie wall unless lawful. Also: does a site with a single optional purpose behind a click need a global banner at all, or does a per-embed gate suffice? Give the reasoned options (plan K1).
4. **Check every policy text against the code, sentence by sentence.** Anything the text promises that the code does not do is a finding (e.g. «si cambiamos estas normas, te lo volvemos a preguntar» while LUZ stores but never compares `policy`). Check the GDPR art. 13 information: controller identity and contact, purposes, legal basis, recipients, transfers, retention, rights (access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction, portability), right to complain to the **AEPD**, and the cookie table (name, provider, purpose, type, duration).
5. **Legal notice (LSSI art. 10)**: whether this site must publish one (economic activity, even indirect: ads, «contrátame», affiliate links) and the minimum content if so; contact address.
6. **Licences and IP**: for every asset, who holds the rights and on what basis it is used — own work, licence (with its conditions: attribution, licence text alongside, share-alike, non-commercial), quotation right (art. 32 LPI: inclusion of fragments for analysis, criticism or teaching, citing source and author, proportionate), or nothing (then it is a finding). Covers the fonts (OFL text must travel with them), LUZ and other code (MIT notice), WordPress images and the credited/uncredited photos (Carnaval, Unsplash, «@DIAZ FARRE» watermark), derivative works (the halftone of a photo is still the photo), screenshots of third-party sites, song lyrics, embedded tweets rendered as static cards, AI-generated images (authorship, platform terms, transparency), TTS voices (Piper voice licences, some non-commercial or research-only). Then the site's own licence: code vs content, with the exceptions for third-party material (plan L4–L9), `LICENSE` files, `<link rel="license">` and the structured-data fields.
7. **Rank by risk to a private individual**: likelihood of complaint or claim × severity (AEPD sanction, takedown, copyright claim) ÷ effort. Say plainly what is a must before launch, what is good practice, and what is not worth doing here. Say when the answer is genuinely uncertain and where the doctrine is split.
8. **Cite your sources**: article and law, AEPD guide section, EDPB guideline, CJEU case, licence clause, with a link to the official text (boe.es, eur-lex.europa.eu, aepd.es, edpb.europa.eu, the licence's canonical URL). Check the current version on the web before citing anything that may have changed; never quote from memory a figure, deadline or article number you have not verified.
