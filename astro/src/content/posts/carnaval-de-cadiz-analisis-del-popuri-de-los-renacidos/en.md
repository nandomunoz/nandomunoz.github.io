## First quatrain: we find out who Los Renacidos are

It speaks directly to the listener, putting the focus on the nature of life, where the only permanent thing is change, a perpetual cycle: “<em>Sabes que la vida nunca, nunca se detiene</em>”. And with the first nod (of many) to <a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her%C3%A1clito#El_fuego">Heraclitus</a> and his idea of fire as the element of transformation.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Sabes que la vida nunca, nunca se detiene</em></p><p><em>Es como una rueda</em></p><p><em>Que en el fuego está girando</em></p><p><em>Gira, vuelve, cambia, se transforma</em></p><p><em>Nace y muere</em></p><p><em>Y hoy es la semilla</em></p><p><em>Que mañana será un árbol.</em></p></blockquote><figcaption>Lyrics: Miguel Ángel García Argüez, «Los renacidos», COAC 2022</figcaption></figure>

And that change is not only felt within us, but in every everyday aspect, material and immaterial, like the sun, the sea, “<em>Ni las olas paran nunca su canción</em>”, the grass, the wrinkles of the skin, the measuring of time “<em>Ni las horas paran</em><em> / Ni los siglos ni los días</em>”. All the more so in the turbulent post-Covid era, where sensibilities are running high “<em>Nunca se detiene</em><em> /El tren furioso de estos tiempos</em>”

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Nunca se detiene</em></p><p><em>El sol cruzando por el cielo</em></p><p><em>Ni las horas paran</em></p><p><em>Ni los siglos ni los días</em></p><p><em>Nunca se detiene</em></p><p><em>El tren furioso de estos tiempos</em></p><p><em>No sé para nunca</em></p><p><em>Ni tu vida ni la mía</em></p><p><em>No para el mar</em></p><p><em>Ni las olas paran nunca su canción</em></p><p><em>Ni el tic-tac</em></p><p><em>Se detiene en el reloj</em></p><p><em>No para la hierba</em></p><p><em>Cuando brota de la tierra</em></p><p><em>Ni se paran los amores</em></p><p><em>Ni la arruga de tu piel</em></p><p><em>Ni los chiquillos que juegan</em></p></blockquote></figure>

The endless circle, unstoppable like the songs of the people of Cádiz that are present in spring. A reference to the change of the carnival date “<em>Que por mayo el milagro / De febrero y las aceras /Y que llego la vida nueva </em>/ El dios Momo está en la hoguera”

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Todo gira y todo vuela</em></p><p><em>Todo corre sin freno</em></p><p><em>Todo pasa y todo queda</em></p><p><em>Que este año por fin</em></p><p><em>Ya reventó la primavera</em></p><p><em>Y ahora Cádiz es de color</em></p><p><em>Porque nada permanece</em></p><p><em>Porque todo se renueva</em></p><p><em>Ave fénix vuela, vuela</em></p><p><em>Que por mayo el milagro</em></p><p><em>De febrero y las aceras</em></p><p><em>Y que llego la vida nueva</em></p><p><em>El dios Momo está en la hoguera</em></p><p><em>Y hay un pueblo que celebra</em></p><p><em>Que por fin aquí no hay quien detenga</em></p><p><em>A las coplas de mi tierra</em></p></blockquote></figure>

The music during this quatrain brims with optimism and vitality. It works as a positive reinforcement of the concept of permanent transformation, of the impossibility of stopping or pausing time, life. It urges you to take it as an advantage, with joy. It invites you to breathe in the change, to live it, to enjoy it. And how does it do it? By having the segundas, the coreaos, accompany the group, who sing each line exquisitely, without stridency, often like faint whispers in the background, with the guitar guiding the whole. The back row is a delight for the ear; if you pay attention, the amount of nuance is almost infinite and never repetitive.

## Second quatrain: when Cádiz is fire

Correct me if I am wrong, but I think this is one of the few times that Cádiz is painted in other colours at Carnival. Miguel does not focus on the typical silver colour or the white of its alleyways. He brings onto his own turf a Cádiz of fire, reddish, but also indigo, bluish, scarlet, like a clean, clear flame.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>No estoy de acuerdo con llamar</em></p><p><em>Taza de Plata</em></p><p><em>A una ciudad que es del color de la escarlata</em></p><p><em>Y el que diga que es de plata</em></p><p><em>Es que no ha mirado bien</em></p><p><em>Es escarlata porque hay fuego en todas partes</em></p><p><em>Fuego en el cielo si el sol renace</em></p><p><em>Fuego si las nubes arden</em></p><p><em>Detrás de San Sebastián</em></p><p><em>Fuegos blancos si la luna</em></p><p><em>Viene a enjuagarse en el mar</em></p><p><em>Fuego y roce en las campanas</em></p><p><em>Fuego en cada gaditana</em></p></blockquote><figcaption>Lyrics: Miguel Ángel García Argüez, «Los renacidos», COAC 2022</figcaption></figure>

Next, that fire does not only colour the most representative elements of Cádiz, but they are fire themselves; the balconies, the summer, passion, the mattress, the voice… are metaphorically part of it.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Y es el incendio azul de estos veranos</em></p><p><em>El fuego que bautiza a mis hermanos</em></p><p><em>Fuego añil en mi balcón</em></p><p><em>Fuego rojo en tu colchón</em></p><p><em>Fuego de oro en tus manos</em></p><p><em>Y es el incendio que desde estos mares</em></p><p><em>El mismito donde se bañó mi madre</em></p><p><em>Fuego grana en tu pasión</em></p><p><em>Fuego negro aquí en mi voz</em></p><p><em>Fuego y fuego que arde</em></p></blockquote></figure>

It closes with three lines that serve as a crutch to reach the fourth “<em>Cádiz…. …</em><em>Es una canción en llamas</em>” Rarely can so much be said with so little. They practically sum up the meaning and the feeling of the renacidos. Cádiz is a song in flames: <strong>song</strong> because it is the way we people of Cádiz live and experience the world. Song as a symbol of joy, of expression, of release… but <strong>in flames</strong>. That is, always alert, passionate, transforming, on the verge of burning, even more so during carnival.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Yo es que mire a donde mire</em></p><p><em>Veo solo fuego en su alma</em></p><p><em>Que Cádiz no es Taza de plata</em></p><p><em>Es una canción en llamas</em></p></blockquote></figure>

## Third quatrain: social criticism and commitment

The guitar changes the rhythm and we move into the most protest-driven quatrain of the popurrí. It reflects on the uncertainty of these times, times in which truths wobble, everything is questioned and everything is turned upside down. <a href="https://www.microfilosofia.com/2020/11/heraclito-volumen-ii-los-contrarios-el.html">Heraclitus returns with the struggle of opposites</a> (fire as the representation of the constant change of the sensible, the representation of a change that never ceases).

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>El mundo esta boca abajo</em></p><p><em>Llegan los nuevos tiempos</em></p><p><em>Y tos los libros de historia</em></p><p><em>Hay que escribirlos de nuevo</em></p><p><em>Lo antiguo vuelvo a nacer</em></p><p><em>Lo nuevo se ha hecho ya viejo</em></p><p><em>Lo que ayer era malo</em></p><p><em>Ahora resulta que es bueno</em></p><p><em>Lo que ayer era sano</em></p><p><em>Hoy por lo visto es veneno</em></p><p><em>Lo lleno ya está vacío</em></p><p><em>Y es el vacío el relleno</em></p></blockquote><figcaption>Lyrics: Miguel Ángel García Argüez, «Los renacidos», COAC 2022</figcaption></figure>

Politicians in carnival have been, are and will be a target of criticism, as well as everyone who holds power or aspires to it. Argüez, in a single word “<em>telepredicadores</em>”, has defined the lawmakers as heralds of ideological-religious truth, as representatives of good and evil, as guardians of the justice of what is right and immaculate.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Los telepredicadores</em></p><p><em>Gritan desde el parlamento</em></p><p><em>Y los que alzaron su voz</em></p><p><em>En nombre de la libertad</em></p><p><em>Se han vuelto tus carceleros</em></p></blockquote></figure>

Then he narrows it down even further, worried about how the far right is occupying the traditional spaces of the working class “<em>Los fascistas hoy recorren</em><em> / Los barrios de los obreros</em>” He describes how the class struggle is deliberately blurred by these groups.

Since the left is more concerned with defining what the left is and is not, they see internal ideological debate as disloyalty and external debate as the enemy. “<em>Y la izquierda con nuevas biblias</em><em>/Impone sus evangelios</em>”. So much tension makes us turn against one another.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Los fascistas hoy recorren</em></p><p><em>Los barrios de los obreros</em></p><p><em>Y la izquierda con nueva biblias</em></p><p><em>Impone sus evangelios</em></p><p><em>Los que piden democracia</em></p><p><em>Son talibanes armados</em></p><p><em>Y el tirano es camarada</em></p><p><em>Y el camarada un tirano</em></p></blockquote></figure>

The role of carnival authors in this political scenario is disappointing and perfunctory for Miguel Ángel “<em>Y en el carnaval los poetas</em><em> /Hoy se han vuelto funcionarios</em>” or simply a job “<em>Los que ayer eran artistas</em><em> /Hoy solo son mercenarios</em>”

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Y en el carnaval los poetas</em></p><p><em>Hoy se han vuelto funcionarios</em></p><p><em>Los que ayer eran artistas</em></p><p><em>Hoy solo son mercenarios</em></p></blockquote></figure>

And he ends with a rallying cry in the face of so much chaos: forget the past and move forward. Or perhaps: do not forget the past, simply pass it through the sieve of transforming fire to create something new, the solution.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Todo cambia nada queda</em></p><p><em>Han llegado nuevos tiempos</em></p><p><em>Tira tus libros de historia</em></p><p><em>Tira tus libros de historia</em></p><p><em>Pa que ardan en el fuego</em></p><p><em>Pa que ardan en el fuego</em></p></blockquote></figure>

## Fourth quatrain: Cádiz as a phoenix

We jump to the next quatrain with a “<em>pa pa pa…”</em> as a link from the back row, which bathes it harmoniously, perfectly in tune, flowing without pause through that transition with no instrument but the voice.

The phoenix spreads its wings, giving back its reflection in the mirror to every “<em>Gaditanos, gaditanas</em>”. Its three millennia, the streets, the children, the destiny…

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Gaditanos, gaditanas</em></p><p><em>Míralos de frente</em></p><p><em>Son igual que el ave fénix</em></p><p><em>Renaciendo siempre</em></p><p><em>Tres mil años y sigue aquí mi gente</em></p><p><em>Con sus vidas</em></p><p><em>Sus calles</em></p><p><em>Sus hijos</em></p><p><em>Su suerte</em></p></blockquote><figcaption>Lyrics: Miguel Ángel García Argüez, «Los renacidos», COAC 2022</figcaption></figure>

A necessary assertion of industry, and especially of Cádiz's shipbuilding, always so punished, but getting back up.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Renaciendo de una industria con futuro incierto</em></p><p><em>De unos barcos</em></p><p><em>Que no regresan a este puerto</em></p><p><em>Golpe a golpe y sigue renaciendo</em></p><p><em>De su pena</em></p><p><em>Sus ruinas</em></p><p><em>Su daño</em></p><p><em>Su sueño</em></p></blockquote></figure>

The following stanzas recall the calamities that the people of Cádiz have historically gone through and how they have managed to recover from them. It warns about a destructive kind of tourism, the rebelliousness of the voices in their coplas and guitars, the danger of fascism tinting its blackness, and poverty as a palpable reality.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Cádiz</em></p><p><em>Renace de explosiones y de maremotos</em></p><p><em>Cádiz</em></p><p><em>Y del turismo ciego que hoy lo arrasa todo</em></p><p><em>Y renacen sus guitarras</em></p><p><em>Sus cantos rebeldes y sus gargantas</em></p><p><em>Siguen renaciendo como antes renacían</em></p><p><em>Del fascismo negro</em></p><p><em>Y de la negra tiranía</em></p><p><em>Y renace en cada mesa</em></p><p><em>Donde se codea la pobreza</em></p><p><em>Y en cada barquilla</em></p><p><em>Cuando nuestros barcos cesan</em></p><p><em>Y renace en cada joven nuestro que regresa</em></p></blockquote></figure>

Then, the last two stanzas spring back up like coils to remind us that “<em>no hay cenizas tristes</em>”, that the past is the springboard to go forward, above all among the young who come back. To end with the very clear life-affirming tribute to Juan Carlos Aragón and his “<em>Cádiz resiste</em>”

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Gaditanos, gaditanas</em></p><p><em>No hay cenizas tristes</em></p><p><em>Que del barro de la historia</em></p><p><em>Siempre renaciste</em></p><p><em>Por eso Cádiz existe</em></p><p><em>Insiste</em></p><p><em>Persiste</em></p><p><em>Cádiz resiste</em></p></blockquote></figure>

## Fifth quatrain: a song to life

The final point and farewell of the popurrí is a plea for life “<em>Y si has vuelto a renacer</em><em> / Agárrate a la vida como un sueño</em>” above all if you have had a stumble along the way.

<figure class="pull"><blockquote lang="es"><p><em>Y si has vuelto a renacer</em></p><p><em>Agárrate a la vida como un sueño</em></p><p><em>Vuelve a ser niño otra vez</em></p><p><em>Y aviva la cantera del deseo</em></p><p><em>Rompe las barreras</em></p><p><em>Celebra cada trago y cada herida</em></p><p><em>Pa que hiervan en tu corazón</em></p><p><em>Las llamas que te llaman a la vida</em></p></blockquote><figcaption>Lyrics: Miguel Ángel García Argüez, «Los renacidos», COAC 2022</figcaption></figure>

Not much more to add, since every line from here on is a joy, a life-affirming fervour, almost danceable at times, an invitation to constant optimism, the personification of Cádiz as an inextinguishable, purifying and motivating being: “<em>Vida nueva</em>”, “<em>Primavera</em>”, “baila en la fuego”, “duerme con el corazón”, “<em>Haz el loco y el amor</em><em>”</em>

Also remembering those who are gone “<em>Y si de lo que queda atrás / Yo guardo algo / Compañero de mi alma /Es recordar la vida eterna / Que a tu lado /Codo a codo yo viví</em>” If you look closely, the choruses add, only during this stanza, the famous line «<em>Creo en el espíritu libre y santo</em>» from Juan Carlos Aragón's Credo.

<strong>In short, a masterpiece of a popurrí that will forever stand on the podium of the great classics of the Cádiz Carnival. The tandem <a href="https://twitter.com/eme_raul">Raúl</a> + <a href="https://twitter.com/cronopiomiguel">Miguel</a> + <a href="https://twitter.com/ComparsaArguez">Group</a> is simply sublime or, as we say in Cádiz: “De pellizco y bellito de punta”.</strong>
