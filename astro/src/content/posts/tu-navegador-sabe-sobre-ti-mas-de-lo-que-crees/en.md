Your digital footprint is the collection of all the data you leave across the web, most of the time without your consent and sometimes with... your consent? By the end of this article I promise you will be more aware of your data on the internet, and we will suggest a few solutions to reduce that handing over of data a little.

## This is you on the internet, and you know it 😀

Picture an ordinary person going about their internet use. Better still, let's give them a made-up name, say, you could call her María Segura. María Segura is a carefree young woman, about 31, who loves football and big-release American films.

Every day she visits a news and gossip site devoted to her favourite football team. She often clicks on links to current-affairs and humour articles that her friends send to one of her 20 WhatsApp groups. María is delighted, because some afternoons she lies on the sofa to watch new-release films; a colleague at work recommended a website where you can watch new-release films streaming for free without having to download anything. She likes the convenience of shopping online from her phone, and she is so passionate about online shops selling good-quality clothes at low prices that sometimes parcels even arrive that she doesn't remember buying :D.

Whenever anyone talks to her about data and privacy on the internet, she shies away, she prefers not to know, and she often says: “Besides, if I have nothing to hide, they can take whatever data they want”, although deep down she is a little worried about her ignorance; even so, she feels a false sense of security knowing that everyone she knows is just as uninformed about this subject as she is.

## What is this digital footprint? 🕵️‍♂️

The type and make of the device you browse from, the date and time of each visit, your language, your battery level, whether you are charging it and therefore how long you have before it dies. These are only a few basic pieces of data that any website can find out about you without any kind of restriction.

In the following table I have put together a classification of some types of data that your browser can collect about you every time you browse the web. The website in question can store them and build your profile with them: this is what we call a “digital footprint”.

## Other, more sophisticated types of data

You may have heard of Google Analytics, a Google service installed on most websites on the internet, which<a href="https://www.redeszone.net/2019/08/06/cookies-propias-cookies-terceros"> identifies, collects and stores all the data mentioned above along with plenty more.</a> All that information gives them statistical, anonymous data (at first sight).

<blockquote><p>The aim of online tracking for organisations, companies, websites and so on is to gain thorough knowledge of their users, their behaviour and preferences.</p><p>This knowledge is used to optimise ease of use and the user experience. It is also used for statistics, personalisation, business and profiling, as well as targeted marketing.</p><cite>Source: <a href="https://www.cookiebot.com/es/seguimiento-online/">Cookiebot.com</a></cite></blockquote>

## Simple things you can do to improve your privacy

Security, both offline and online, is never 100%. If you leave the window of your house open when you go on holiday, it is more likely that someone can burgle you; in the same way we can try to <strong><strong>make it harder for the thieves of your data</strong></strong>.

### IKAY project

As a programmer with many years behind me working in the world of the internet, <a href="https://nandomunoz.github.io/ikay">it was easy for me to develop the IKAY project (I know about you</a>). IKAY is a website where I show you in a simple way the <em><a href="#webapi">most basic data</a></em> that any website can find out about you, together with recommendations to avoid leaking it as far as possible.

### Tips on using WhatsApp

WhatsApp is a social network that has come into our lives in a very natural way. For that very reason we need to be more careful about how we use it and, above all, not click on just any link that is sent to us.

The newspaper <a href="https://www.abc.es/tecnologia/consultorio/abci-cinco-consejos-para-mejorar-seguridad-cuenta-whatsapp-202002130207_noticia.html">ABC gives us five tips to improve the security</a> of our WhatsApp:

- Use two-step verification
- Set up your profile's privacy
- Check the groups settings
- If you have a modern phone, use Touch ID or Face ID
- Update this app and all the apps on your phone

### How to avoid harmful advertising and tracking on the internet

When a service on the internet is free, you are the product. The most widely used business model on the web is advertising, but it is not always known for its ethics. <a href="https://www.pcworld.es/tutoriales/internet/evitar-rastreo-online-3788194/">PCWorld</a> advises us to follow these points to avoid the tracking of our profile that is used to show us advertising:

- Turn off personalised ads in Google's apps
- Block third-party cookies
- Turn on ‘Do Not Track’ in your browser
- Browse privately
- Use anti-tracking browser extensions
- Use a secure browser (don't use Internet Explorer)

### Buying online is safe, but we need to take certain precautions

The juiciest piece of data for those who love what belongs to others on the internet is our credit card, which is why I always recommend putting a secure intermediary in between. In my view <a href="https://www.paypal.com/es/webapps/mpp/personal">creating a PayPal account</a> is best, <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://www.adslzone.net/2017/02/12/mas-seguro-pagar-paypal/">especially when you buy for the first time</a> on a new website.

<blockquote><p>With the rise in online purchases and e-commerce platforms, attempts to steal our data and scams by cybercriminals are also growing.</p><cite>Source: <a href="https://www.osi.es/es/actualidad/blog/2019/06/19/como-comprar-online-y-no-caer-en-el-intento">OSI</a></cite></blockquote>

- Have an up-to-date antivirus installed.
- If you use a phone/tablet, buy from the official app
- Buy over your home wifi, avoid connecting to public networks
- Check the online shop's key information: who they are, registered address, payment methods, delivery policies and returns policies
- If you have doubts, never buy there
- If it's too good to be true, it's surely a fraud

### Social networks are a reflection of the data you give them every day

<strong>Never say on social networks anything you wouldn't say in public in real life.</strong>

<em>Would you shout in the middle of the town square that you are going on holiday, or that you will be out of the house for three hours because you are at a restaurant? </em>So why are you surprised that digital criminals are on the lookout and read your social networks to gather information about you?

Would you tell a stranger in the street your political ideology, how you feel today, when you met your boyfriend, that you are pregnant, the day, month, year and time of your birth, your full name with surnames…? So why do you give that information to Facebook?

## Conclusion

The internet is a wonderful place to contribute knowledge, learn, get informed, communicate or shop. That is why we should not be afraid of it, but be aware of the value of our data. There are <a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reglamento_General_de_Protecci%C3%B3n_de_Datos">people and institutions who watch over its security</a>; let's give it the importance it deserves and we will be able to handle the challenges of the 21st century.
